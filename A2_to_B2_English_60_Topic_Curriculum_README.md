# 🚀 A2 → B2 English Speaking Roadmap

> A practical **60-topic English curriculum** designed to take you from approximately **A2 to B2** through vocabulary, collocations, grammar, listening, speaking, reading, and writing.

---

## 🎯 The Goal

This roadmap is not about memorizing 60 speeches.

The goal is to become able to:

- 🗣️ speak spontaneously about familiar and abstract topics
- 👂 understand normal English conversations, videos, interviews, and discussions
- 📖 read articles and longer texts with increasing independence
- ✍️ write clear, organized B1/B2-level texts
- 🧠 use vocabulary in **phrases and real sentences**, not isolated words
- 💬 explain opinions, give reasons, compare ideas, discuss problems, and defend a viewpoint

### The progression

| Stage | Main ability |
|---|---|
| **Topics 1–15** | 🟢 Describe things |
| **Topics 16–30** | 🟡 Explain things |
| **Topics 31–45** | 🟠 Discuss things |
| **Topics 46–60** | 🔴 Analyze, argue, and speculate |

# 🧩 How to Study EVERY Topic

Use the same system for all 60 topics.

## The 8-Step Topic System

```text
1. 🗺️ Topic Map
       ↓
2. 🔑 Core Keywords
       ↓
3. 🧱 Vocabulary in Context
       ↓
4. 💬 Collocations & Useful Phrases
       ↓
5. ⚙️ Topic-Relevant Grammar
       ↓
6. 👂 Listening + 📖 Reading
       ↓
7. 🗣️ Speaking
       ↓
8. ✍️ Writing + Review
```

The purpose is to turn:

> **word → phrase → sentence → idea → conversation**

Before studying vocabulary, spend **5–10 minutes** breaking the topic into smaller areas.

### Your rule

**Don't start with a dictionary. Start with the topic structure.**

# 3️⃣ Learn Vocabulary in Context

❌ Don't learn:

> **debt = money you owe**

Instead learn:

### Word
**debt**

### Meaning
Money that you owe to another person or organization.

### Common combinations
- get into debt
- pay off debt
- be in debt
- reduce debt
- personal debt

### Example
> Many people get into debt because they spend more than they earn.

### Personal sentence
> I think people should avoid unnecessary debt.

This makes the vocabulary usable.

---

# 4️⃣ Learn Collocations & Phrases

For every topic, aim for:

- **25–35 core keywords**
- **20–30 collocations**
- **15–20 useful sentence patterns**

### Example: Money

**Saving**
- save money
- put money aside
- build up savings
- reach a savings goal

**Spending**
- spend money on
- cut down on spending
- reduce unnecessary expenses
- live within your means

**Debt**
- get into debt
- pay off debt
- take out a loan
- repay a loan

**Investment**
- invest in something
- make a profit
- suffer a loss
- earn a return

### Golden rule

> **Learn phrases, not isolated words.**

---

# 5️⃣ Study Grammar Through the Topic

Do not study every grammar structure for every topic.

Choose **2–4 grammar structures** that help you express the ideas of that topic.

### Example: Money

**Modals**

> People **should** save some of their income.

**First conditional**

> If people **don't control their spending**, they **may get into debt**.

**Second conditional**

> If I **earned more money**, I **would save more**.

**Cause & effect**

> Rising prices **can lead to** financial difficulties.

The grammar should become something you **use**, not just something you can explain.

---

# 6️⃣ Listening & Reading

## 👂 Listening — 2–3 pieces

For each topic:

### First listen
Listen without subtitles/transcript.

Ask:

> What is the main idea?

### Second listen
Use English subtitles/transcript.

Collect:

- useful phrases
- collocations
- interesting sentence patterns
- pronunciation you want to copy

### Third listen
Listen again without subtitles.

Then try **shadowing**:

> Listen → pause → repeat → copy the speaker's rhythm and pronunciation.

---

## 📖 Reading — 1–2 texts

Choose an article or suitable text about the topic.

While reading, identify:

- main idea
- important arguments
- useful vocabulary
- useful phrases
- examples
- the writer's opinion

Don't translate every word.

Try to understand the **message first**.

---

# 7️⃣ Speaking Practice

This is the most important stage for your B2 goal.

Prepare **10–15 questions** for every topic.

Use three difficulty levels.

### 🟢 Easy

> What do you usually spend money on?

> Do you save money?

### 🟡 Intermediate

> Why do some people struggle to save money?

> How can people reduce their expenses?

### 🔴 B2

> Does having more money necessarily make people happier?

> Should financial education be taught at school?

> What should governments do about rising living costs?

---

## 🎤 Speaking progression

Don't immediately try to speak for 10 minutes.

### Round 1
**30 seconds**

Answer simply.

### Round 2
**1 minute**

Add a reason and example.

### Round 3
**3 minutes**

Use:

> Opinion → Reason → Example → Second point → Contrast → Conclusion

### Round 4
**5–10 minutes**

Discuss the topic naturally.

### Round 5
**Challenge yourself**

Ask:

> "What if someone disagrees with me?"

Then respond.

This is how you develop B2-level spontaneous speaking.

---

# 8️⃣ Writing Practice

For every topic, do two levels of writing.

## ✍️ Short writing

Write **100–150 words**.

Focus on:

- correct grammar
- vocabulary
- clear sentences

## ✍️ B2 writing

Write **200–300 words**.

Use:

```text
Introduction
      ↓
Main idea / Argument 1
      ↓
Example / Explanation
      ↓
Argument 2 / Opposing view
      ↓
Your position
      ↓
Conclusion
```

### Example question

> **Some people believe that young people should learn how to manage money at school. To what extent do you agree?**

# 📓 The 5-Page Notebook System

For every topic, create five sections.

## PAGE 1 — 🗺️ Topic Map

Break the topic into 5–7 subtopics.

## PAGE 2 — 🔑 Core Vocabulary

**25–35 words**

For each important word:

> Word → simple meaning → example

## PAGE 3 — 💬 Collocations & Phrases

**20–30 collocations**

and

**15–20 useful sentence patterns**

## PAGE 4 — ⚙️ Grammar & Sentence Patterns

Write the **2–4 grammar structures** you need for the topic.

Then create your own examples.

## PAGE 5 — 🧠 My Ideas

Write:

- my opinion
- my experience
- examples from my life/country
- arguments
- opposing arguments
- predictions
- questions I couldn't answer

This final page turns **English knowledge into your English**.

---

# 🧠 Vocabulary Mastery Levels

Not every word needs to become active vocabulary immediately.

Use these four levels:

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Recognize** | You understand it when you see/hear it |
| 🟡 **Understand** | You can explain it simply |
| 🟠 **Use** | You can naturally use it in a sentence |
| 🔴 **Flexible** | You can use different forms and collocations |

### Your B2 goal

Aim for your most important topic vocabulary to reach **🟠 Use**.

You do **not** need every word to reach 🔴 Flexible.

---

# 🔥 The Golden Vocabulary Formula

Whenever you meet a new word:

```text
WORD
  ↓
MEANING
  ↓
COLLOCATION
  ↓
EXAMPLE SENTENCE
  ↓
YOUR OWN SENTENCE
  ↓
YOUR OWN IDEA
  ↓
USE IT IN SPEAKING
```

### Example

**debt**

↓

**pay off debt**

↓

> She is trying to pay off her debt.

↓

> I want to avoid getting into unnecessary debt.

↓

> I think taking on some debt can be reasonable if it helps someone buy a home or start a business.

Now you are not just memorizing **debt**.

You're learning to **think with the word**.

# ⏱️ Recommended Schedule Per Topic

A good target is **4–5 days per topic**.

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | 🗺️ Topic map + vocabulary + collocations |
| **Day 2** | ⚙️ Grammar + listening |
| **Day 3** | 👂 Listening + 📖 reading + shadowing |
| **Day 4** | 🗣️ Speaking + vocabulary recall |
| **Day 5** | ✍️ Writing + final speaking test + review |

If a topic is difficult, take **6–7 days**.

Do not rush simply to finish the 60 topics.

---

# 🔁 Spaced Review

After finishing a topic, review it:

- **1 day later**
- **7 days later**
- **30 days later**

During review, don't reread everything.

Instead:

### Close your notebook.

Try to remember:

- 20 vocabulary words
- 10 collocations
- 5 sentence patterns
- 5 important ideas

Then speak for **3–5 minutes**.

If you can't remember something, reopen the notes.

---

# 📊 Topic Completion Test

Don't mark a topic as "completed" just because you studied it.

Mark it complete only when you can answer **YES** to most of these:

- [ ] I know 25–35 important words.
- [ ] I know 20+ useful collocations.
- [ ] I can use at least 10 phrases naturally.
- [ ] I understand the key grammar for this topic.
- [ ] I can understand the main idea of normal English content about it.
- [ ] I can read an article about it.
- [ ] I can speak for 5 minutes without a script.
- [ ] I can answer unexpected questions.
- [ ] I can give reasons and examples.
- [ ] I can compare two viewpoints.
- [ ] I can explain advantages and disadvantages.
- [ ] I can write 200–300 words about it.
- [ ] I can paraphrase when I don't know a word.

---

# 🏆 Your Ultimate Goal

Don't aim for:

> ❌ **"I finished 60 English topics."**

Aim for:

> ✅ **"If someone suddenly starts talking to me about one of these topics, I can understand them, express my opinion, ask questions, explain my ideas, give examples, and continue the conversation without preparing a speech."**

That is much closer to **real B2 communication**.
